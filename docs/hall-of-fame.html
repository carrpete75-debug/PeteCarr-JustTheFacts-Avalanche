Avalanche Hall of Fame

The Avalanche Desk’s own Hall: 13 members. It starts with the 6 players whose numbers hang in the Ball Arena rafters, then 4 strong cases, then 2 active stars nominated now and inducted when they retire, and one entry in the Villain wing. Stats are for the Colorado era only (1995–96 on), regular season unless marked playoffs. Quebec Nordiques years are listed separately.

As of Thu Oct 1, 2026 · Active players’ stats are through Wed Sep 30, 2026 · Unofficial: this is a fan page, not a team hall of fame

Retired numbers

The six numbers retired by the Avalanche: 19 Sakic, 21 Forsberg, 23 Hejduk, 33 Roy, 52 Foote, 77 Bourque. Wayne Gretzky’s 99 is retired league-wide.

Joe Sakic #19

Center · Avalanche 1995–2009 · Quebec Nordiques 1988–95

No. 19 retired · Oct 1, 2009HHOF 2012🏆 1996 · 2001

  • Captain for all 14 Colorado seasons; he first wore the C in Quebec in 1992–93.
  • 1996 Conn Smythe: 18 goals and 34 points, including 6 game-winning goals.
  • 2000–01 Hart Trophy, Lady Byng and Lester B. Pearson Award (54 goals, 118 points). In Game 7 of the 2001 Final he scored the power-play goal that made it 3–0, then handed the Cup straight to Ray Bourque.
  • Colorado: 870 GP, 391 G, 624 A, 1,015 PTS · playoffs 160 GP, 77 G, 177 PTS. Quebec: 508 GP, 234 G, 626 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Sakic) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · HHOF 2012 · retired-number dates

Peter Forsberg #21

Center · Avalanche 1995–2004, 2007–08, 2010–11 · Quebec Nordiques 1994–95

No. 21 retired · Oct 8, 2011HHOF 2014🏆 1996 · 2001

  • 1994–95 Calder Trophy with Quebec (15 G, 50 PTS in 47 games).
  • 116 points (30 G, 86 A) in Colorado’s first season, 1995–96.
  • 2002–03 Hart Trophy and Art Ross Trophy (29 G, 77 A, 106 PTS).
  • Colorado: 544 GP, 202 G, 503 A, 705 PTS · playoffs 134 GP, 56 G, 153 PTS. Came back twice, for 9 games in 2007–08 and 2 in 2010–11.

Sources: NHL.com (Forsberg) · HHOF 2014 · retired-number dates

Patrick Roy #33

Goaltender · Avalanche 1995–2003 · later head coach (from 2013)

No. 33 retired · Oct 28, 2003HHOF 2006🏆 1996 · 2001

  • Arrived from Montreal during the 1995–96 season. He had 16 wins and 3 shutouts in the 1996 Cup run.
  • 2001 Conn Smythe, the third of his career and still the most by any player: 16 playoff wins and 4 shutouts. He also won the 2001–02 Jennings Trophy.
  • Retired in 2003 as the NHL’s all-time wins leader (551 career wins).
  • Colorado: 478 GP, 262–140–65, 37 SO, .918 SV% · playoffs 133 GP, 81–52, 18 SO. Won the 2014 Jack Adams Award in his first season as Avs head coach.

Sources: NHL.com (Roy) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · Hockey-Reference (Roy coaching, 2014 Jack Adams) · retired-number dates

Ray Bourque #77

Defense · Avalanche 2000–01 (traded from Boston during 1999–2000)

No. 77 retired · Nov 24, 2001HHOF 2004🏆 2001

  • Played 21 seasons in Boston (1979–2000), then won the Cup in his 22nd and final NHL season.
  • After Game 7 on June 9, 2001, Sakic took the Cup from Gary Bettman and handed it to Bourque, who lifted it first.
  • Five Norris Trophies and the 1980 Calder Trophy, all with Boston.
  • Colorado: 94 GP, 15 G, 58 A, 73 PTS · playoffs 34 GP, 5 G, 19 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Bourque) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · CBC (2001 Cup) · retired-number dates

Adam Foote #52

Defense · Avalanche 1995–2004, 2008–11 · Quebec Nordiques 1991–95

No. 52 retired · Nov 2, 2013🏆 1996 · 2001

  • Captain from 2009 to 2011, succeeding Sakic.
  • Played three seasons in Columbus (2005–08) and returned to Colorado late in 2007–08. He assisted on Tanguay’s Cup-winning goal in Game 7 of the 2001 Final.
  • Colorado: 760 GP, 48 G, 173 A, 221 PTS · playoffs 158 GP, 7 G, 40 PTS. Quebec: 207 GP, 38 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Foote) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · AvalancheDB captains · retired-number dates

Milan Hejduk #23

Right wing · Avalanche 1998–2013

No. 23 retired · Jan 6, 2018🏆 2001

  • 2002–03 Maurice “Rocket” Richard Trophy with 50 goals (98 points).
  • Spent all 14 of his NHL seasons with Colorado. He was captain in 2011–12, then handed the C to Gabriel Landeskog.
  • Assisted on Sakic’s goal in Game 7 of the 2001 Final. Won Olympic gold with the Czech Republic in 1998.
  • Colorado: 1,020 GP, 375 G, 430 A, 805 PTS · playoffs 112 GP, 34 G, 76 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Hejduk) · nhl.com/avalanche (No. 23) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score

Strong cases

Rob Blake #4

Defense · Avalanche 2001–06 (acquired from Los Angeles during 2000–01)

HHOF 2014🏆 2001

  • Joined midseason and won the Cup three months later: 19 points (6 G, 13 A) in 23 playoff games in 2001.
  • Won the 1998 Norris Trophy with Los Angeles.
  • Colorado: 322 GP, 62 G, 146 A, 208 PTS · playoffs 68 GP, 16 G, 43 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Blake) · HHOF 2014 · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Alex Tanguay #40

Left wing · Avalanche 1999–2006, 2013–16

🏆 2001Also wore No. 18 (2003–04, 2005–06)

  • Scored twice in Game 7 of the 2001 Final, including the Cup-winning goal, and added an assist. He had 21 points (6 G, 15 A) that spring.
  • Returned for a second stint (2013–16) before a 2016 trade to Arizona.
  • Colorado: 598 GP, 167 G, 321 A, 488 PTS · playoffs 83 GP, 18 G, 50 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Tanguay) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Gabriel Landeskog #92

Left wing · Avalanche 2011–present · captain since Sept 4, 2012

🏆 2022Active

  • No. 2 pick in 2011 and 2011–12 Calder Trophy winner. Named captain at 19 years, 286 days, the youngest in NHL history at the time.
  • Captained the 2022 Cup team (22 points in 20 playoff games). Knee problems then kept him out of the 2022–23 through 2024–25 regular seasons; he returned for 5 games in the 2025 playoffs.
  • 2025–26: 35 points in 60 games, plus 11 in 13 playoff games. He won both the Masterton Trophy and the Mark Messier Leadership Award, the first player ever to win both.
  • Colorado (through Sep 30, 2026): 799 GP, 263 G, 344 A, 607 PTS · playoffs 87 GP, 34 G, 82 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Landeskog) · NBC Sports (captaincy) · nhl.com/avalanche (Masterton + Messier)

Valeri Kamensky #13

Left wing · Avalanche 1995–99 · Quebec Nordiques 1991–95

🏆 1996Nordiques carryover

  • Moved with the franchise from Quebec. In 1995–96 he scored 38 goals and 85 points, then had 22 points (10 G, 12 A) in 22 games on the 1996 Cup run.
  • Assisted on a Sakic power-play goal in Game 6 of the 1996 West Final, when Colorado eliminated Detroit.
  • Colorado: 289 GP, 106 G, 155 A, 261 PTS · playoffs 56 GP, 24 G, 58 PTS. Quebec: 171 GP, 60 G, 153 PTS (wore Nos. 17 and 31 there).

Sources: NHL.com (Kamensky) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · Hockey-Reference 1995–96 · 1996 WCF Game 6 box score · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Villain wing

For the player Avs fans loved and everyone else loved to hate.

Claude Lemieux #22

Right wing · Avalanche 1995–99 (traded to New Jersey early in 1999–2000)

🏆 1996Villain wing

  • May 29, 1996, West Final Game 6: his check from behind on Detroit’s Kris Draper (fractured jaw, broken nose, facial cuts) drew a major and a game misconduct. The NHL called it a “cheap shot,” suspended him for the first two games of the Final and fined him $1,000. The Avs–Red Wings rivalry took off from there.
  • Won the 1996 Cup with Colorado, one of his four (1986 Montreal, 1995 and 2000 New Jersey). He was the 1995 Conn Smythe winner with New Jersey.
  • Scored 13 goals in 17 playoff games in 1997.
  • Colorado: 297 GP, 106 G, 106 A, 212 PTS · playoffs 62 GP, 24 G, 55 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Lemieux) · New York Times (1996 suspension) · 1996 WCF Game 6 box score
