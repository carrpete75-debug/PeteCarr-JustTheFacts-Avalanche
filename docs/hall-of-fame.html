Avalanche Hall of Fame

The Avalanche Desk’s own Hall: 22 cards for 21 people. It starts with the 6 players whose numbers hang in the Ball Arena rafters, then 9 strong cases, 3 coaches, 2 executives and 2 active stars nominated now and inducted when they retire. Joe Sakic is in twice, as a player and as an executive. Stats are for the Colorado era only (1995–96 on), regular season unless marked playoffs. Quebec Nordiques years are listed separately.

As of Thu Oct 1, 2026 · Active players’ and Bednar’s numbers are through Wed Sep 30, 2026 · Unofficial: this is a fan page, not a team hall of fame

Retired numbers

The six numbers retired by the Avalanche: 19 Sakic, 21 Forsberg, 23 Hejduk, 33 Roy, 52 Foote, 77 Bourque. Wayne Gretzky’s 99 is retired league-wide.

Joe Sakic #19

Center · Avalanche 1995–2009 · Quebec Nordiques 1988–95

No. 19 retired · Oct 1, 2009HHOF 2012🏆 1996 · 2001Also inducted as an executive ↓

  • Captain for all 14 Colorado seasons; he first wore the C in Quebec in 1992–93.
  • 1996 Conn Smythe: 18 goals and 34 points, including 6 game-winning goals.
  • 2000–01 Hart Trophy, Lady Byng and Lester B. Pearson Award (54 goals, 118 points). In Game 7 of the 2001 Final he scored the power-play goal that made it 3–0, then handed the Cup straight to Ray Bourque.
  • Colorado: 870 GP, 391 G, 624 A, 1,015 PTS · playoffs 160 GP, 77 G, 177 PTS. Quebec: 508 GP, 234 G, 626 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Sakic) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · HHOF 2012 · retired-number dates

Patrick Roy #33

Goaltender · Avalanche 1995–2003 · later head coach (from 2013)

No. 33 retired · Oct 28, 2003HHOF 2006🏆 1996 · 2001

  • Arrived from Montreal during the 1995–96 season. He had 16 wins and 3 shutouts in the 1996 Cup run.
  • 2001 Conn Smythe, the third of his career and still the most by any player: 16 playoff wins and 4 shutouts. He also won the 2001–02 Jennings Trophy.
  • Retired in 2003 as the NHL’s all-time wins leader (551 career wins).
  • Colorado: 478 GP, 262–140–65, 37 SO, .918 SV% · playoffs 133 GP, 81–52, 18 SO. Won the 2014 Jack Adams Award in his first season as Avs head coach.

Sources: NHL.com (Roy) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · Hockey-Reference (Roy coaching, 2014 Jack Adams) · retired-number dates

Ray Bourque #77

Defense · Avalanche 2000–01 (traded from Boston during 1999–2000)

No. 77 retired · Nov 24, 2001HHOF 2004🏆 2001

  • Played 21 seasons in Boston (1979–2000), then won the Cup in his 22nd and final NHL season.
  • After Game 7 on June 9, 2001, Sakic took the Cup from Gary Bettman and handed it to Bourque, who lifted it first.
  • Five Norris Trophies and the 1980 Calder Trophy, all with Boston.
  • Colorado: 94 GP, 15 G, 58 A, 73 PTS · playoffs 34 GP, 5 G, 19 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Bourque) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · CBC (2001 Cup) · retired-number dates

Alex Tanguay #40

Left wing · Avalanche 1999–2006, 2013–16

🏆 2001Also wore No. 18 (2003–04, 2005–06)

  • Scored twice in Game 7 of the 2001 Final, including the Cup-winning goal, and added an assist. He had 21 points (6 G, 15 A) that spring.

  • Colorado: 598 GP, 167 G, 321 A, 488 PTS · playoffs 83 GP, 18 G, 50 PTS.

Gabriel Landeskog #92

Left wing · Avalanche 2011–present · captain since Sept 4, 2012

🏆 2022Active

  • No. 2 pick in 2011 and 2011–12 Calder Trophy winner. Named captain at 19 years, 286 days, the youngest in NHL history at the time.
  • Captained the 2022 Cup team (22 points in 20 playoff games). Knee problems then kept him out of the 2022–23 through 2024–25 regular seasons; he returned for 5 games in the 2025 playoffs.
  • 2025–26: 35 points in 60 games, plus 11 in 13 playoff games. He won both the Masterton Trophy and the Mark Messier Leadership Award, the first player ever to win both.
  • Colorado (through Sep 30, 2026): 799 GP, 263 G, 344 A, 607 PTS · playoffs 87 GP, 34 G, 82 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Landeskog) · NBC Sports (captaincy) · nhl.com/avalanche (Masterton + Messier)

Valeri Kamensky #13

Left wing · Avalanche 1995–99 · Quebec Nordiques 1991–95

🏆 1996Nordiques carryover

  • Moved with the franchise from Quebec. In 1995–96 he scored 38 goals and 85 points, then had 22 points (10 G, 12 A) in 22 games on the 1996 Cup run.
  • Assisted on a Sakic power-play goal in Game 6 of the 1996 West Final, when Colorado eliminated Detroit.
  • Colorado: 289 GP, 106 G, 155 A, 261 PTS · playoffs 56 GP, 24 G, 58 PTS. Quebec: 171 GP, 60 G, 153 PTS (wore Nos. 17 and 31 there).

Sources: NHL.com (Kamensky) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · Hockey-Reference 1995–96 · 1996 WCF Game 6 box score · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Claude Lemieux #22

Right wing · Avalanche 1995–99 (traded to New Jersey early in 1999–2000)

🏆 1996

  • Came to Colorado for the first season in 1995–96 and scored 39 goals (71 points) as the Avalanche won the Cup in their first year.
  • Four Stanley Cups (1986 Montreal, 1995 and 2000 New Jersey, 1996 Colorado), and the 1995 Conn Smythe with New Jersey.
  • Scored 13 goals and 23 points in 17 playoff games in 1997. His check on Detroit’s Kris Draper in Game 6 of the 1996 West Final brought a two-game suspension, which kept him out of the first two games of the Final.
  • Colorado: 297 GP, 106 G, 106 A, 212 PTS · playoffs 62 GP, 24 G, 55 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Lemieux) · New York Times (1996 suspension) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup

Adam Deadmarsh #18

Right wing · Avalanche 1995–2001 · Quebec Nordiques 1994–95

🏆 1996

  • Quebec’s first-round pick (14th overall) in 1993. Joined the 1996 Cup run with 17 points (5 G, 12 A) in 22 playoff games.
  • Career-best 33 goals and 60 points in 1996–97. Five straight 18-plus-goal seasons in Colorado.
  • Went to Los Angeles in the 2001 trade that brought Rob Blake to Colorado. He later returned to the Avs as a video/development coach (2009) and assistant coach (2011–12).
  • Colorado: 405 GP, 129 G, 142 A, 271 PTS · playoffs 82 GP, 22 G, 55 PTS. Quebec: 48 GP, 9 G, 17 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Deadmarsh) · Wikipedia (trade, coaching) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Chris Simon #12

Left wing · Avalanche 1995–96 · Quebec Nordiques 1992–95

🏆 1996Lindros-trade piece

  • Joined Quebec from Philadelphia as part of the 1992 Eric Lindros trade.
  • Gave the 1995–96 team its muscle: 250 penalty minutes, plus 16 goals and 34 points. He won the Cup that spring and took it home to Wawa, Ontario.
  • Traded to Washington with Curtis Leschyshyn after the 1996 Cup. He died on March 18, 2024, aged 52.
  • Colorado: 64 GP, 16 G, 18 A, 34 PTS, 250 PIM · playoffs 12 GP, 1 G, 3 PTS. Quebec: 82 GP, 8 G, 22 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Simon) · Globe and Mail (Lindros trade, where they went) · Wikipedia · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Mike Ricci #9

Center · Avalanche 1995–98 (traded to San Jose Nov 1997) · Quebec Nordiques 1992–95

🏆 1996Lindros-trade piece

  • Came to Quebec from Philadelphia in the 1992 Lindros trade and scored 78 points (27 G, 51 A) in his first Nordiques season.
  • A key two-way center in the 1996 playoffs: 17 points (6 G, 11 A) in 22 games.
  • Traded to San Jose on Nov 21, 1997. The first-round pick Colorado got in that deal became Alex Tanguay.
  • Colorado: 131 GP, 19 G, 44 A, 63 PTS · playoffs 39 GP, 8 G, 23 PTS. Quebec: 208 GP, 72 G, 165 PTS.

Chris Drury #37

Center · Avalanche 1998–2002

🏆 20011999 CalderAlso listed as No. 18 in 2001–02

  • 1998–99 Calder Trophy (20 G, 44 PTS). That made him the first player to win both the Hobey Baker Award (Boston University, 1998) and the Calder. Quebec had drafted him 72nd overall in 1994.
  • Known for clutch playoff goals: 11 playoff game-winning goals in four Colorado postseasons, and 11 goals in 23 games on the 2001 Cup run.
  • As a boy he pitched Trumbull, Connecticut to the 1989 Little League World Series title. Traded to Calgary on Oct 1, 2002.
  • Colorado: 314 GP, 85 G, 137 A, 222 PTS · playoffs 80 GP, 26 G, 50 PTS.

Sources: NHL.com (Drury) · Wikipedia (Hobey/Calder, LLWS, trade) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · Hockey-Reference sweater numbers

Coaches wing

Records are NHL regular season with Colorado unless noted. Ties existed through 2003–04 and overtime losses from 1999–2000.

Marc Crawford

Head coach · Avalanche 1995–98 · Quebec Nordiques 1994–95

🏆 19961995 Jack Adams (with Quebec)

  • Won the 1995 Jack Adams Award in Quebec, his first NHL season as a head coach (30–13–5 in the lockout-shortened 1994–95), then moved with the franchise to Denver.
  • Coached the 1996 champions: 47–25–10 (104 points) in the regular season and 16–6 in the playoffs.
  • His teams finished first in their division in each of his three Colorado seasons, including 49–24–9 (107 points) in 1996–97.
  • Colorado: 246 games, 135–75–36 (W–L–T) · playoffs 29–17. Quebec: 30–13–5 · playoffs 2–4.

Sources: Hockey-Reference (Crawford coaching record) · NHL Records: 1996 Cup

Bob Hartley

Head coach · Avalanche 1998–2002 (through 31 games of 2002–03)

🏆 2001

  • Finished first in the division in all four of his full seasons in Colorado.
  • 2000–01: 52–16–10–4 (118 points) and the Presidents’ Trophy, then 16–7 in the playoffs and the 2001 Stanley Cup.
  • Won the Jack Adams Award later, in 2014–15, with Calgary.
  • Colorado: 359 games, 193–108–48–10 (W–L–T–OTL) · playoffs 49–31.

Sources: Hockey-Reference (Hartley coaching record) · 2001 Final Game 7 box score · nhl.com/avalanche (Presidents’ Trophies)

Jared Bednar

Head coach · Avalanche 2016–present

🏆 2022Active

  • Hired in August 2016 after Patrick Roy resigned. His first team went 22–56–4, last in the NHL, and Colorado has made the playoffs in every season since.
  • 2021–22: 56–19–7 (119 points), then 16–4 in the playoffs and the 2022 Stanley Cup. In 2025–26 his team won the Presidents’ Trophy at 55–16–11 (121 points).
  • The winningest coach in franchise history. On Sept 30, 2026, the day of the 2026–27 opener, he signed a four-year extension through 2030–31.
  • Colorado (through Sep 30, 2026): 783 games, 446–262–75 (W–L–OTL) · playoffs 60–41.

Sources: Hockey-Reference (Bednar coaching record) · NHL.com (extension) · NBC Sports/AP (hire, franchise wins)

Executive wing

Pierre Lacroix

General manager & president · GM 1994–2006 (Quebec, then Colorado) · team president through May 10, 2013

🏆 1996 · 2001HHOF 2023 (Builder)

  • A longtime player agent, he was hired as Nordiques GM on May 24, 1994 and moved with the club to Colorado. His teams won nine straight division titles, an NHL record, plus two Presidents’ Trophies and the 1996 and 2001 Cups.
  • Built the Cup teams by trade: Patrick Roy and Mike Keane from Montreal (Dec 6, 1995), Ray Bourque and Dave Andreychuk from Boston (Mar 6, 2000), and Rob Blake and Steve Reinprecht from Los Angeles (Feb 21, 2001). He also matched a large offer sheet to keep Joe Sakic.
  • Named 1996 NHL Executive of the Year by The Hockey News. He gave up the GM job on May 12, 2006 and stayed on as president until 2013.
  • Record as GM: 473–259–106–30. He died Dec 13, 2020, aged 72, and was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame as a builder in 2023.

Sources: HHOF (Lacroix, 2023) · nhl.com/avalanche (record, titles) · AP (Sakic offer sheet) · NHL statement via Pro Hockey News (trade dates)

Joe Sakic

Executive · Front office since Mar 25, 2011 · EVP/GM 2013–22 · president of hockey operations since July 11, 2022 · GM again since June 2026

🏆 20222021–22 GM of the YearAlso inducted as a player ↑

  • Joined the front office in 2011 as executive advisor and alternate governor, and was named executive vice president on May 10, 2013. He oversaw the rebuild from last in the NHL in 2016–17 (22–56–4) to the 2020–21 Presidents’ Trophy.
  • 2022 Stanley Cup as GM (56–19–7, then 16–4 in the playoffs), plus the 2021–22 Jim Gregory General Manager of the Year Award, a first for the club. The draft picks and trades behind that team included Cale Makar, Mikko Rantanen, Devon Toews, Nazem Kadri and Artturi Lehkonen.
  • The only person in NHL history to win the Stanley Cup as captain and as GM of the same organization (1996 and 2001 as captain, 2022 as GM).
  • Became president of hockey operations on July 11, 2022, and took the GM duties back after Chris MacFarland left for Nashville on June 2, 2026.

Sources: nhl.com/avalanche (roles, captain-and-GM first) · NHL Records (Jim Gregory Award) · ESPN (2022 promotion, roster moves) · nhl.com/predators (Lehkonen/Toews trades)
