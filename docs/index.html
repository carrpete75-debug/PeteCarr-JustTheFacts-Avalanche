Record

2–0–04 pts · 4th in Central

Two games into 2026–27 (W–L–OT): 14 goals for and 5 against. Colorado was idle again Tuesday and slid from 2nd to 4th in the NHL’s division order. Utah won 5–3 at New Jersey to reach 3–1–0 (6 pts in 4 games). Minnesota picked up a point in a 3–2 overtime loss at Buffalo (2–0–1, 5 pts), level with Winnipeg (2–0–1). Every team above the Avs has played at least one more game. The 14 goals through two games tie a franchise record set by the 1992–93 Quebec Nordiques (Avalanche.com). Last season (2025–26): 55–16–11, 121 points, first in the Central and first in the NHL. Playoffs: reached the Western Conference Final and were swept 4–0 by Vegas (AP).

Last game · Sat Oct 3 vs Blues

Martin Necas scored twice and added an assist, Nathan MacKinnon had three assists, and the Avalanche routed the St. Louis Blues 6–1 at Ball Arena. Cale Makar opened the scoring at 4:09 of the first and Necas made it 2–0 later in the period. Then Josh Manson, Artturi Lehkonen, Nicolas Roy and Necas again blew it open with four goals in the second. Mackenzie Blackwood stopped 25 of 26 shots; Jordan Binnington allowed all six Colorado goals on 36 shots. Three stars: Necas, MacKinnon, Lehkonen.

Since then: The Avs held a long full-team practice Tuesday before flying to Winnipeg. O’Connor and L’Heureux were both full participants; it was O’Connor’s first full-go skate. “I think they’re both going to play in these two games,” Bednar said (NHL.com). L’Heureux told Colorado Hockey Now he expects to make his Avalanche debut tonight, and Bednar said he’ll go back to 12 forwards and 6 defensemen as soon as one of them is ready. The power play also changed after an 0-for-4 night against St. Louis: Nazem Kadri moved up to the top unit and Gabriel Landeskog moved to the second (CHN).

Next: Tonight at Winnipeg (5:30 PM MDT, ALT), Thursday at Calgary (7:00 PM MDT, ALT / KTVD), then Saturday vs Toronto (5:00 PM MDT, NHL Network / ALT).
